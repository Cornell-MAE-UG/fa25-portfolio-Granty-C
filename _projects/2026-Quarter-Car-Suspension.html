Two masses, two springs

The body moves on the suspension and the wheel moves on the tire. That second motion makes it possible to compare tire contact as well as body comfort.

Coil and damper

The body is 300 kg, the wheel assembly is 60 kg, and the tire stiffness is 190 kN/m. The coil rate is 16 kN/m. Baseline damping is 1,000 N·s/m.

All displacements are measured from static ride height. Suspension compression q is wheel displacement minus body displacement. A positive suspension force F acts upward on the body and downward on the wheel.

ms z̈s = F mu z̈u = kt(r − zu) − F

Sealed air spring

Compression shrinks the chamber volume and raises its absolute pressure. Static lift balances body weight. The gas force used in the simulation is the increase from that equilibrium lift.

V = V0 − Aeq kair = n p0Ae2 / V0

At 7.5 L with a 0.015 m² effective area and polytropic exponent of 1.30, the tangent rate is 11.60 kN/m. Increasing volume lowers the rate at the same static load.

What changed the ride

The 50 mm bump

At 0.20 s crossing time, the nonlinear air case lowered RMS body acceleration from 1.113 to 1.009 m/s² and settled 0.42 s earlier. It used 1.27 mm more suspension travel and its minimum tire load fell from 1.116 to 0.965 kN.

Frequency changes the ranking

On a 5 mm sinusoidal road, the linear air model has slightly more body motion at 0.9 Hz and less at 1.1 Hz. Both models have a wheel-hop mode near 9 Hz, where tire load can vary by about 3 kN while the body moves only about 1 mm.

Match the rates

At 5.439 L the air tangent and coil both have a rate of 16 kN/m. The linear traces then coincide. At that volume the nonlinear air spring predicts more body motion on the same bump than the coil, 31.23 mm versus 28.50 mm.

More damping settles faster

In the air model, raising damping from 650 to 1,600 N·s/m cut settling from 3.91 to 1.34 s. It also raised RMS body acceleration from 0.834 to 1.367 m/s².

Model limits. The comparison covers vertical motion at one corner with a sealed, constant-area air chamber and a linear damper and tire. A tire load at or below zero flags where the continuous-contact assumption breaks down.

Reproduce the study

I focused on the Python simulation and the comparison of the body and tire responses for our three-person group. The model integrates four mechanical states with fourth-order Runge–Kutta at 0.5 ms steps. Halving the time step changed each baseline metric by less than 0.1%.
